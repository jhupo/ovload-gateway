# ADR 0005: Procedural public homepage

Status: accepted. Date: 2026-09-19.

The user explicitly requested direct homepage implementation after the asset
review, superseding the review-only delivery scope. The public route owns a
full-screen Three.js scene and a PrimeVue login overlay. The existing Vue, Aura,
font, language and theme systems remain the UI foundation.

All scene assets are procedural geometry, particles and thin connected lines.
Hands have a palm, five fingers and a short tapered wrist without an arm. Surface
topology, short connections and directional fibers establish depth; particles
converge from global traffic and dissolve after connection. The colorful cartoon programmer
faces the window with their back toward the camera and occupies roughly 15% of
the viewport height at the lower center. The room and MacBook share this scale.
A continuous task stream links the laptop to a common center. A quiet flat particle
world map remains behind four large curved trunks and their merging branches. A
small three-orbit relay core and low-density center hand mark the exact convergence
coordinate. Thirty-two pre-sampled edge and upper-field sources add five to ten
converging lanes at a time, then retain three representative lanes after arrival.
The first twenty captures do not reuse a source path. Twenty retained capture
groups bound the ambient scene. When the limit is exceeded,
the oldest route scatters and fades before its resources are disposed. OpenAI,
Grok and Gemini marks exist only on the active gathering hand.

Three.js owns perspective, particle interpolation, curves and rendering. GSAP
owns one finite globe/unfold/gather intro, a separate ambient loop and an
interruptible login gesture. Vue owns routes and semantic dialog visibility;
PrimeVue owns the focus trap. Login is available throughout the intro. Reduced
motion immediately exposes the final layout and usable controls. Inactive and
hidden scenes pause, and teardown releases all WebGL and animation resources.

Three.js is a runtime dependency, its declarations a development dependency.
The renderer is lazy imported. No images, remote models, provider traffic or
credentials are needed. Authentication remains an explicitly unavailable planned
API; forms do not simulate successful login or registration.
