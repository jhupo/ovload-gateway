# ADR 0005: Procedural public homepage

Status: accepted. Date: 2026-09-19.

The user explicitly requested direct homepage implementation after the asset
review, superseding the review-only delivery scope. The public route owns a
full-screen Three.js scene and a PrimeVue login overlay. The existing Vue, Aura,
font, language and theme systems remain the UI foundation.

All scene assets are procedural geometry, particles and thin connected lines.
Hands have a palm, five fingers and a short tapered wrist without an arm. Surface
topology, short connections and directional fibers establish depth; particles
converge from global traffic and dissolve after connection. The colorful cartoon programmer
faces the window with their back toward the camera and occupies roughly 15% of
the viewport height at the lower right. The room and MacBook share this scale.
A quiet flat particle
world map with warm country nodes remains behind a tree of connected traffic and a bounded
reservoir of one hundred merging branches. Thirty branches are visible when the
scene settles and the remaining access paths grow in over three minutes. One upper
stem, fourteen irregular boughs and fifty-six canopy limbs establish the primary hierarchy.
The tree is rooted left of the room. Its substantial lower trunk has a volumetric
particle surface and nineteen twisting fibers. Eleven tapering roots have secondary
forks and active traffic. Hundreds of terminal sprays form a layered teal/blue
particle canopy with restrained gold accents. Particle buffers are allocated once;
formation and gentle motion run in the shader under the existing scene clock.
All traffic traces this tree through the relay coordinate on the stem, without a
separate center emblem or fixed hand. Thirty-two pre-sampled edge and upper-field sources
add five to ten lanes to a nearby canopy limb, then retain three representative lanes
after arrival. The first twenty captures do not reuse a source path. Twenty retained
capture groups bound the ambient scene. When the limit is exceeded, the oldest
route scatters and fades before its resources are disposed. OpenAI, Grok and Gemini
marks exist only on the active gathering hand.

The room is offset farther right and is no longer connected to the tree by a laptop
stream. All access branches belong to the tree. At most six small particle butterflies
appear, fly along long curved paths in the side clearings, fade and reappear along
new paths. A closer mobile camera and a more central tree position increase the
canopy's visual presence. Every twenty-six seconds, a random batch of one to six
reusable leaf instances falls from sampled canopy positions with small staggered
delays, settles at the ground and fades away. Their GPU animation uses
the existing scene clock and lifecycle. Shared, small spatial displacement gives nearby lines and particles gentle motion while
keeping outer sources fixed. Distance-resampled traffic follows its limb, bough
and stem to the relay, then continues toward a canopy destination in both directions.
The character, hands and laptop use one room scale and coherent rear-view
occlusion. Inset colorless depth geometry prevents particle surfaces showing
through each other without introducing a visible solid model. Light mode uses
gray-purple trunks, mauve branches and fuller rose-pink/lilac foliage; slate map
particles and copper regional nodes remain separate background layers. Falling
leaves match the canopy palette. The dark tree retains teal, blue and muted gold.

Three.js owns perspective, particle interpolation, curves and rendering. GSAP
owns one finite globe/unfold/gather intro, a separate ambient loop and an
interruptible login gesture. Vue owns routes and semantic dialog visibility;
PrimeVue owns the focus trap. Login is available throughout the intro. Reduced
motion immediately exposes the final layout and usable controls. Inactive and
hidden scenes pause, and teardown releases all WebGL and animation resources.

Three.js is a runtime dependency, its declarations a development dependency.
The renderer is lazy imported. No images, remote models, provider traffic or
credentials are needed. Authentication remains an explicitly unavailable planned
API; forms do not simulate successful login or registration.
